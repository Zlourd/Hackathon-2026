What You Could Have Lost

Your data is vulnerable and up for grabs.

Most of what companies know about you, you never told them. It is gathered quietly as you browse, search, shop and move around, then packaged, traded and sold. Here is how it happens, and what you can do about it.

In this guide
1

How your data gets collected

Eight everyday ways tech companies learn about you.

🍪

Cookies

Small files that let a site recognise your browser. Third-party cookies, set by ads and widgets embedded in pages, let a company follow you across many sites.

👀

Hidden trackers

Many websites and apps secretly include small bits of software from other companies. It records what you look at, tap and buy, even if you never log in.

Also called pixels or SDKs.
🧩

Fingerprinting

Your screen size, fonts, graphics card, language and timezone form a pattern that is often unique. It identifies you without cookies, so clearing them does not help.

📍

Location

GPS, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cell towers and your IP address reveal where you are. Location data from apps has been bought and sold by data brokers.

🔍

Searches, views & purchases

What you search, watch and buy shows your interests, worries, income and plans, whether or not you meant to share them.

🔑

Accounts & logins

Signing in links your activity across devices. Even a scrambled (hashed) email address can be used to match you between sites and apps.

🗃️

Data brokers

Little-known companies combine public records, purchases, app data and browsing into detailed profiles, then sell access to advertisers and others.

📺

Smart TVs & speakers

Many smart TVs recognise what is on screen to build viewing profiles, and voice assistants process what they hear. Settings can limit both.

2

From clues to a profile

Three kinds of information. Only the first is something you handed over.

WHAT YOU TELL THEM

Name, email, birthday, address and phone number, usually when you sign up or check out.

WHAT THEY WATCH

Pages, searches, clicks, time spent, purchases, location history and device details.

WHAT THEY GUESS

Income range, age group, family situation, health interests, politics, even “likely to buy a car this month”.

⚠️Guesses can be wrong, and some are sensitive, but they still decide what you see and what you are offered.
3

Why it matters

A single signal is harmless. Years of them add up.

🏷️

Different offers

Personalisation can change the ads, deals and sometimes prices you are shown.

💧

Profiles leak

Breaches and people-search sites expose collected data, and copies are hard to pull back.

❓

Mistakes stick

You can be labelled wrongly, with no easy way to see or fix it.

🕰️

It lasts

Data is stored, resold and combined for years.

4

How to protect your data

Start with the first column. Every step cuts down what is collected.

QUICK WINSabout 10 minutes
  • ✓Use a browser that blocks trackers by default (Firefox, Brave, Safari) or add a reputable content blocker.
  • ✓Turn on Global Privacy Control, so sites that honour it treat you as opted out of data sale or sharing.
  • ✓Limit app permissions: location “While using” or “Never”, and no microphone, contacts or photos unless needed.
  • ✓Reset or switch off your phone’s advertising ID, and auto-delete your account activity history.
LEVEL UPan evening
  • ✓Use a password manager and two-factor authentication, so one breach cannot spread.
  • ✓Use email aliases for sign-ups, and avoid “Sign in with…” buttons that link your activity across sites.
  • ✓Keep browsing separate: different browser profiles for banking, social media and everything else.
  • ✓Prefer search, maps and messaging apps that collect less. For chats, look for end-to-end encryption.
GO FURTHERwhen you want more
  • ✓Opt out of data brokers and people-search sites, or use a removal service. Profiles can reappear, so repeat.
  • ✓Use a VPN or Tor to hide your IP address. A VPN only shifts trust to its provider.
  • ✓Turn off content recognition on smart TVs and limit voice assistants.
  • ✓Add noise with an obfuscation tool such as Odysseus (next section).
5

Where Odysseus fits

Obfuscation: adding noise instead of trying to disappear.

WHAT IT DOES
  • ✓Gives every site a consistent decoy persona: fake browser details, language, timezone and location.
  • ✓Runs decoy searches and visits, so trackers learn interests that are not yours.
  • ✓Sends the Global Privacy Control signal while the decoy is on and masking your browsing.
WHAT IT CANNOT DO
  • —Hide your IP address. Pair it with a VPN if that matters to you.
  • —Stop sites you are logged into from knowing it is you.
  • —Erase what has already been collected, or prove that a real ad company’s profile of you has changed. The tracker view and Polyphemus are simulations.
🧭Obfuscation makes you harder to profile accurately. It is not invisibility, so combine it with the steps in section 4.
6

Your rights

Laws differ by location, but many places give you real control.

⚖️

EU and UK (GDPR)

Ask what a company holds about you, correct it, have it deleted in many cases, and object to profiling and direct marketing. You can complain to your regulator.

🏛️

California and other US states

Laws such as the CCPA and CPRA let you see what is collected, delete or correct it, and opt out of its sale or sharing. Other states have similar laws.

✉️

How to use them

Look for “Privacy” or “Your privacy choices” in a site’s footer. Send access or deletion requests in writing and keep a copy.

General information, not legal advice.

7

Myths that keep people exposed

Four things many people believe that are not quite true.

MYTH
Incognito mode makes me anonymous.
REALITY
It only stops your browser saving history on your device. Websites, your school or employer, and your internet provider can still see what you do.
MYTH
Clearing my cookies resets me.
REALITY
Fingerprints, logins and device IDs can still identify you, and trackers simply set new cookies.
MYTH
A VPN makes me anonymous.
REALITY
It hides your IP address from sites, but the provider sees your traffic and logins still identify you.
MYTH
Ads only use what I click.
REALITY
Most signals are passive: what you view, how long you linger, where you are and what device you use.
